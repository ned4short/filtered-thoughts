The Judgment of Solomon: Why an Endorsement Doesn’t Establish Truth

The judgment of Solomon became a defining story of his wisdom, helping establish the reputation that would reach beyond Israel.

In 1 Kings 3:16–28, two women claimed the same living child. Each insisted she was the mother. But what if Solomon had simply endorsed either woman?

He could have rewarded a lie and separated a child from his mother. Even if he had chosen correctly, the question would remain: How did he know?

Instead, Solomon proposed dividing the child. One woman accepted that outcome; the other pleaded that the baby be given to her rival rather than killed. Her willingness to surrender her claim to save the child revealed what competing assertions had concealed.

The account ends with all Israel hearing of his judgment and recognizing his God-given wisdom. Later passages describe that reputation reaching other nations.

The lesson reaches beyond an ancient courtroom: authority carries a responsibility to examine claims before endorsing them.

Confidence, repetition, and status cannot substitute for a sound basis for judgment.

A Christian or political leader’s endorsement does not establish truth.

Wisdom requires looking beyond the claim and finding a way to test it.
